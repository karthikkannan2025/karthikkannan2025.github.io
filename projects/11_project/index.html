Wind Turbine Blade Cleaning Drone

Exploring a tethered cleaning system and the loads that determine its feasibility

The question behind the concept

I started this project with a question: could an aerial robot make wind turbine blade cleaning practical enough to justify the equipment and operating effort? My interest is in both the engineering and the business case—what would it take to clean a blade effectively, and how would we demonstrate that doing so is worthwhile?

The concept uses water and cleaning solution supplied from the ground. Carrying a useful fluid supply onboard would add a substantial payload, so I began exploring a tethered aircraft with a cleaning head instead. Onshore turbines are the initial focus; offshore operation is a longer-term possibility.

The hose became the central design problem

When I considered operating around 150 m, the question changed from choosing a drone to understanding the entire suspended system. The aircraft would have to deal with the hose, water inside it, power or data lines, and forces transmitted through those connections. A reel controls payout, but does not by itself remove the weight of an unsupported hanging hose from the aircraft.

That leaves a real architecture trade: how much load can the aircraft carry, and would an external support arrangement be needed? I am treating the support geometry and load path as unresolved design work rather than assuming that a larger motor or a ground winch solves the problem.

Cleaning and flight control have to work together

Spray cleaning raises questions about nozzle reaction force, pressure loss, wind disturbance, and maintaining distance from the blade. Brushing adds contact force and torque, which makes controlling the vehicle near the surface a different problem. Cleaning effectiveness also needs to be evaluated alongside the effect on blade coatings.

The useful next experiments would isolate those questions: characterize a candidate hose and nozzle, measure the loads, and evaluate cleaning on representative samples before committing to a full aircraft. The design needs evidence about the cleaning process as well as the flying platform.

Where it stands

This is a concept and design study, with no cleaning-flight demonstration or verified energy-recovery result yet. The aircraft size, support arrangement, and cleaning method remain open. For me, the value is learning to define a system from its hardest constraints and to connect technical feasibility with an actual operational need.
