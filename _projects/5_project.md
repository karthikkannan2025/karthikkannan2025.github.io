## A practical reason to build a robot

This idea started with a repetitive household task: getting a trash can down a long or steep driveway and back again. I wanted to explore whether a separate robotic base could move an ordinary wheeled can, and what the mechanism would need to do before adding autonomy.

## Sharing the load with the can

The concept uses a motorized chassis and a **linear actuator to engage and tilt the can**, allowing its existing wheels to share the load. That makes the attachment mechanism, center of gravity, and traction part of the same design problem. The robot needs to hold the can securely while negotiating the surface and slope of a driveway.

Early work has included chassis and drive-system CAD, actuator and clamping concepts, and parts sourcing. The first useful prototype milestone is basic remote-controlled movement with a representative load. Stability on uneven ground and reliable engagement matter before adding automatic navigation.

## What remains to be developed

Trash-can recognition, obstacle avoidance, curb placement, and eventual charging or garage integration are future features. They depend on a mechanical platform that can first move and release the can consistently. I am also interested in whether the chassis could support other small hauling tasks, but that remains a design ambition rather than a demonstrated capability.

This project is an opportunity to connect mechanical design with the practical demands of a household robot: handling an imperfect environment, recovering from a failed pickup, and making the mechanism easy to use.
