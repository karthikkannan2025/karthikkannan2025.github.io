<h2>Summary</h2>
<p>
  The Smart Watch Drone project began as an experiment in miniaturization
  and portability. Inspired by the idea of seamlessly integrating a drone
  into everyday life, the goal is to design a compact, foldable UAV
  housed in a smartwatch form factor. This project explores how consumer
  wearables can double as mobile robotics platforms, blending convenience,
  novelty, and practical functionality.
</p>

<h2>Concepts</h2>
<p>
  The Smart Watch Drone is designed as a wrist-worn device that deploys
  into a fully functional micro quadcopter. The core idea is to merge
  everyday wearables with drone technology, creating a system that can
  launch instantly for quick photos or environmental scans. The drone’s
  arms fold neatly into the watch strap, inspired by slappy rulers, so
  the device maintains a compact and wearable form when not in use.
</p>
<p>
  When deployed, the arms lock into position with small brushless
  motors mounted at the ends, which double as both propellers and strap
  tightening points. The watch body houses a lightweight flight
  controller, battery pack, and a small onboard camera. A minimal
  touchscreen provides direct control for simple flight modes, while
  Bluetooth or Wi-Fi connectivity links the drone to a smartphone app
  for extended features like live video feed or image capture. With a
  planned flight endurance of around two minutes, the system emphasizes
  portability and spontaneity rather than long-duration missions.
</p>
<p>
  The goal is to enable everyday, casual use cases: a quick aerial
  selfie, a brief panoramic shot on a hike, or a fast overhead check of
  surroundings. By focusing on these lightweight applications, the
  design avoids overcomplication and keeps the wearable comfortable
  while still delivering a unique and practical drone experience.
</p>


<h2>Existing Solutions</h2>
<p>
  Early prototypes of watch-sized drones have been demonstrated, most
  notably the Nixie drone, which pioneered the idea of a wearable flying
  camera. However, such concepts never advanced beyond initial
  demonstrations due to technological limitations in power density,
  miniaturization, and durability. This project aims to revisit the
  concept with modern advances in microelectronics, lightweight materials,
  and efficient motor control systems, making the design more viable and
  user-friendly. This project never took off the ground and it ended its patent as of 2021.
</p>
