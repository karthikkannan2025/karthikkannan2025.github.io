## Starting with the packaging constraint

The Smart Watch Drone is a concept for a small flying camera that folds into a wrist-worn form. I was interested in the challenge of making the same assembly work as both a wearable object and a quadcopter. A quick photo or short overhead view is the intended use, rather than a long-duration mission.

## The mechanical idea

I explored arms that fold into the strap and lock into position for flight, with the central body housing the battery, flight controller, and camera. The appeal is compact storage; the difficulty is fitting the components while preserving rotor clearance, structural stiffness, and a repeatable deployed geometry.

Those requirements compete with comfort and weight on the wrist. A mechanism that looks compact when folded still has to place the motors correctly and remain secure when deployed. This is the main design tension I want to understand before shrinking the electronics further.

## Power and electronics remain open

The original concept targeted roughly **two minutes of flight**, but that is an aspiration, not measured endurance. Battery capacity, motor selection, camera power, and the mass of the folding mechanism would determine whether it is attainable. A sufficiently small flight-computer PCB is still an unresolved part of the concept.

My intended development path is to study the deployment mechanism and component layout at a larger scale before attempting a wearable prototype. No flight-tested smartwatch drone has been built for this project yet. It is an exercise in how aggressively a useful idea can be constrained by packaging, power, and manufacturability.
