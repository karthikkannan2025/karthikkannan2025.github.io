My work has moved between structural analysis, rapid aircraft development, and flight control verification. Across those settings, I keep finding myself at the connection between **the physical system, the software, and the evidence that tells us whether it works**.

These pages go into how I approach the work: the tools I build, the tests I help run, and what I learn when the result differs from the expectation.

## Boeing · Understanding the system deeply

**GNC Engineer 2 · Everett, WA · April 2026–Present**

On the 777X Primary Flight Function team, most of my work is in test and verification, with contributions across requirements and safety. A test starts with understanding what must be demonstrated, then finding the scenario and signals that can demonstrate it. I work across Psim, the Integrated Test Vehicle's live hydraulics, and on-aircraft testing, and build Python tools to make scenario handling and reporting more consistent.

## Mach Industries · Following a test through

**Flight Test Engineer · Huntington Beach, CA · October 2025–March 2026**

At Mach, I supported an aircraft program that went from concept to first flight in **three months**. I led selected tests, worked with communications and camera streams, and analyzed aircraft data. The pace made it especially important to know what a test was supposed to answer and to follow through when the aircraft or test setup did something unexpected.

## Collier Aerospace · Turning engineering methods into tools

**Structural/Software Engineer · Raleigh, NC · May 2024–October 2025**

At Collier, I worked on composite and metallic structures using HyperX, FEMAP, and NASTRAN. Much of my software work involved translating structural mechanics methods into Python so the team could compare designs and document the analysis. Aircraft-specific spar cutouts were one example of a design choice where geometry, weight, and structural behavior needed to be considered together.
